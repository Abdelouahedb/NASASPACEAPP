Method & data

NABD follows one water system from storage to use with the NASA–ISRO NISAR L-band radar: the surface of Al Massira, Morocco's second-largest reservoir, and the Doukkala farmland its releases irrigate. Radar sees through cloud, day and night, every 12 days.

Data

  • NISAR Level-2 GCOV (provisional): HH and HV backscatter, terrain-corrected. Study areas are cut on NASA's side with Harmony.
  • One orbit track (044, ascending), so every pass has the same viewing geometry.
  • NASA HLS (Landsat + Sentinel-2) optical images on clear days, used only to check the radar.
  • Official reservoir fill rates from published ministry figures (sources below).

Methods

  • Water: speckle filter, then fixed thresholds (HH −15 dB, HV −24 dB) for dark water connected to the reservoir.
  • Farmland: each location's radar history; rules separate water, buildings, trees, seasonal growth and bare soil.
  • Validation: optical NDVI gives independent labels. A random forest on radar only is scored with 5-fold spatial cross-validation (2 km blocks) against the rules and a single-date threshold.
  • Patterns: k-means++ on each location's signal shape and level, with a stability score from two reruns.

Limitations

  • NISAR products are provisional. The reservoir's main refill (Jan–May 2026) happened before the available passes.
  • Wind can roughen the lake surface; HV is used because it is less sensitive.
  • Green in the dry season strongly suggests irrigation but includes some non-irrigated orchards.
